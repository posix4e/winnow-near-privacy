Winnow NEAR privacy

What the Winnow NEAR research build shares, with whom, and when. Apart from TestFlight itself, nothing here happens until you turn on other coins and allow the services listed below.

Effective October 10, 2026 · Applies to the Winnow NEAR app (com.btcswift.near) distributed through TestFlight.

Winnow NEAR is the Winnow Bitcoin wallet plus a NEAR account and NEAR Intents, which let it hold USDC and other coins, swap Bitcoin, and pay people on other networks, and a MobileCoin account for MOB and eUSD, to pay and be paid by Signal and Fire users. Everything the Bitcoin side does is described in the Winnow privacy policy, which applies to this build as well. This page covers only what the NEAR features add.

Like Winnow, this build has no account with us, no analytics, no advertising SDK and no app-managed crash reporting. We do not run a server for it.

Before you allow anything

Until you tap Hold dollars and other coins and then Allow, the app sends nothing to the services below. The consent screen lists each service by address. If that list changes in an update, the app asks again before using it.

On your device

  • Your NEAR account is derived from your wallet's recovery words (SLIP-0010, m/44'/397'/0'), the same account other NEAR wallets derive from those words. The signing key is computed only when you approve an action with Face ID or your passcode, and it is not stored separately.
  • Swap records and NEAR activity are kept in an encrypted file in the app's storage.
  • Addresses on other networks that you save for people stay on the device with the rest of your contacts. Sharing your own card includes your NEAR account ID.
  • Verification of NEAR block headers and transaction results happens on the device and sends nothing extra.
  • Your MobileCoin account is derived from the same recovery words (m/44'/866'/0'), the account Signal and Fire derive from them. Its keys are derived when you open it with Face ID or your passcode and are kept in memory only while the app is in front.
  • Your Ethereum address is derived from the same recovery words (m/44'/60'/0'/0/0). It only holds USDC on its way to Fire's desk. Its key is computed only when you approve a conversion with Face ID or your passcode.
  • Receipts. A receipt for a MobileCoin payment you received is a link to explorer.fire.co that you choose to share. It carries the payment's block, the values that open its amount, your MobileCoin address, a signature by your account's key and, only if you turn it on for that receipt, your @handle. Making one sends nothing. Everything is after the # in the link, which browsers do not send to the server; whoever you give the link to can read it.
  • Notifications are made on the device. A background check reads the state of money in flight (swaps, desk orders, Ethereum sends) from the services named below and shows a notification when one finishes. Nothing goes to a push service.

What leaves the device

NEAR nodes

rpc.mainnet.fastnear.com (FastNEAR), near.drpc.org (dRPC), rpc.mainnet.near.org (NEAR Foundation), and archival-rpc.mainnet.fastnear.com for older blocks.

They see your NEAR account ID, the balances and contracts the app reads, any transaction you submit, and your connection's IP address (or your gateway's exit, when you route through your own Tor gateways).

When: while the app is open, every two minutes, every 30 seconds while a swap runs, and before each action.

1Click swap service

1click.chaindefuser.com, the NEAR Intents swap service.

Quote requests carry the coins and amounts, your NEAR account ID, the recipient's address on their network, a refund address (a fresh address of your wallet or your NEAR account), and your IP address. Status checks name the swap's deposit address. The service holds the money for the minute or so a swap or payment takes.

When: when you ask for a price, confirm a swap or payment, request to be paid in another coin, and while a swap is in progress.

MobileCoin

node1.prod.mobilecoinww.com and fog.prod.mobilecoinww.com (MobileCoin), and fog-rpt-prd.namda.net (Signal's, named by Android users' addresses).

They see your connection's IP address (or your gateway's exit) and the requests the app makes. Balances come from MobileCoin's Fog service, which runs in Intel SGX secure enclaves. The app checks each enclave's attestation before using it, and the enclaves are built so the operator cannot read which coins are yours. A payment you send is submitted to MobileCoin's consensus servers; amounts and parties are hidden on MobileCoin's ledger.

When: while your MobileCoin account is open, and when you send.

Fire's directory

fire-directory.fly.dev, run by Fire.

It sees the @handle or Fire link code you look up, and your IP address. Each lookup is signed with a new throwaway key, so lookups are not linked to each other or to your wallet.

If you claimed a handle in the Fire app with the same recovery words, the app can show it. It reads your profile and changes its two switches (whether people can find your handle, and whether a lookup also gives your Ethereum address) with the device key Fire derives from those words, so the directory links those requests to your handle, as it does for the Fire app. Making an anonymous share link asks it for a code tied to that key. The app never claims a handle and never changes the addresses the directory holds.

When: lookups when you send to a Fire @handle or a Fire link that names one; your profile when you tap "Check for a Fire handle", change a switch or make a share link.

Fire's swap proxy

fire-grpc-proxy.fly.dev, run by Fire, which passes requests to MobileCoin's DEQS exchange.

It sees which coin you want to swap for which, a small proof of work the app solves to be let in, and your IP address. It does not see your address or the amount you enter. The swap itself is a MobileCoin transaction like any other.

When: only when you ask for a price to swap MOB and eUSD.

Bank transfers: Fire's bridge and Iron

fire-iron-bridge.fly.dev, run by Fire, which passes requests to Iron (a MoonPay company).

Your requests are signed with the device key Fire derives from your recovery words, so they reach the same records as the Fire app. The bridge and Iron see your email address, your Ethereum address, the bank account you add for cash-outs (name, routing and account numbers, phone and address) and your IP address. Iron's identity check runs on Iron's own pages, which the app cannot read; Winnow keeps none of it on the device.

When: only when you open "Bank" with Face ID or your passcode and use it.

Fire's desk

fire-market-maker.fly.dev, run by Fire.

When you buy or sell eUSD, the desk sees your Ethereum address, your MobileCoin address, the amount, and your IP address. Buying, it takes the USDC with a permit you sign and pays eUSD to your MobileCoin address, or returns the USDC to your Ethereum address if it can't. Selling, you send it eUSD and sign a binding that lets its escrow pay USDC only to your Ethereum address.

When: only when you buy or sell eUSD, and while that order is running.

Prices

Dollar values come from the prices 1Click already sends with its token list. MOB's price comes from api.coingecko.com.

CoinGecko sees your IP address (or your gateway's exit) and that a MOB price was asked for; nothing about your wallet.

When: while your MobileCoin coins are on screen, at most every five minutes.

Ethereum nodes

ethereum-rpc.publicnode.com and eth.drpc.org.

They are asked for your Ethereum address's balances, its transaction count, fees and receipts, and see that address and your IP address. A transaction you send from that address (USDC, ETH, or moving USDC into your balance) goes to both of them, and from there to Ethereum, where it is public like every Ethereum transaction.

When: only once your Ethereum address is in use (buying or selling eUSD, or opening it), while the app shows it, and when you send from it.

Bridge operators

Bitcoin and some other coins inside NEAR Intents are held by bridge operators: the POA bridge (operator custody), Omni (NEAR's validators) or HOT (HOT's signers), labelled in the app. A bridge sees the Bitcoin address it pays out to and the address deposits come from.

When: when Bitcoin moves in or out, and when a payment is delivered on another network.

Public blockchains

NEAR transactions, including transfers inside NEAR Intents, are public and tied to your NEAR account. Bitcoin deposits to swap addresses, and payouts on other networks, are public on those chains. Anyone watching the swap service's addresses may link them to each other and to your NEAR account.

When: every swap, payment or transfer.

Apple iCloud (optional)

If iCloud is available, swap records and NEAR activity sync between your own devices through the app's CloudKit container. They are encrypted on the device with a key kept in your iCloud Keychain, so Apple stores them without being able to read them. Encrypted wallet backup works as described in the Winnow policy.

When: after the app refreshes, while iCloud is signed in.

Apple TestFlight

Because this build is distributed through TestFlight, Apple collects the crash reports, feedback and usage data that TestFlight collects, under Apple's terms, and shares some of it with us (for example, your feedback and the name and email you were invited with).

When: as TestFlight does for any beta app.

Who we are and limits

Winnow NEAR is a research build. It runs on real networks with real money, and every action is capped at 100,000 sats or $100. We do not sell or share data. Apart from what TestFlight passes on, we do not receive the information above: it goes from your device to the services named, under their own policies.
